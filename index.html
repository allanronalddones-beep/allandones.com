Why This Site Exists

What it's for, and what you can actually take from it.

Most rotating equipment knowledge lives in spreadsheets on one engineer's laptop. It gets lost when they change jobs. This site publishes some of mine as working tools — free, no sign-up, running entirely in your browser.

Check a vendor's numbers

When a supplier quotes a TDH and BHP, you need an independent calculation to judge it against. That's what the tools do — compute the requirement, then compare.

For engineers starting out

Every tool shows its formulas and assumptions on screen. The point isn't to hand you an answer — it's to show the derivation so you can defend it in a review.

Nothing leaves your browser

No accounts, no uploads, no analytics on the tools. Your process data stays on your machine — which matters when the inputs are client-confidential.

A caution worth stating plainly: these tools are engineering aids, not a substitute for discipline judgement or a stamped calculation. Verify every input against your project datasheets and P&IDs, and confirm the reference points match your case before using any output in a design decision.

Construction support15%
Measured by: Factory Acceptance Test and Joint Engineering Inspection first-pass acceptance rate, and site technical query response time.
Change management10%
Measured by: Management of Change cycle time, and the proportion of change surfaced at FEED rather than during construction — where the cost of correction is roughly an order of magnitude higher.
Stakeholder management5%
Measured by: client RFI and technical query response against the agreed service level.

These are the measures the work is held to, stated as method rather than as project figures. Actual performance data belongs to the projects and clients concerned and is not published here.

Engineering judgement The client placed a hold on all HPPT compressor control system documents, arguing that a specification required PLC control and that the proprietary control system was not on the approved vendor list. Changing it at that stage would have hit both cost and schedule, and the control documents interfaced heavily with the plant DCS. On review, the specification cited was neither attached to the purchase order nor cross-referenced in any technical specification or design basis document — and the compressor was a special type, sealless with active magnetic bearings and VFD-driven, whose proprietary control system had been developed specifically to suit it.
