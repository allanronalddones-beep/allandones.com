How to trust the numbers

Engineering documents fail in a specific way: a plausible number with no traceable origin gets copied forward until someone builds it. Every quantitative block in this guide therefore carries one of three tags, and they mean different things.

Derived

Computed inside this guide from first principles. You can follow every step and reproduce it. Errors here are mine and are traceable.

Standard

Quoted from a named clause of a code or standard, cited inline. Confirm the edition your project mandates — values move between editions.

Verify

A representative magnitude, not a read table value. Never carry one of these into a deliverable without replacing it from the handbook or your project basis of design.

Why the Verify tag exists rather than a confident-looking table

CLTD, SCL, CLF, shading coefficients and climatic design data all live in copyrighted ASHRAE tables that could not be opened while writing this. Reproducing them from memory to four significant figures would look authoritative and be untrustworthy — the worst combination in a technical document. Instead the method is exact, the magnitudes are defensible, and every one of them is an input box you overwrite.

Doha design basis

These inputs drive every worked example in the guide. They open on values representative of Doha and of QCS-compliant Gulf construction. Overwrite them with your project basis of design and the calculations re-solve.

The colour factor K is a specification promise, not a discount

K = 0.65 for a light-coloured surface will cut your roof load noticeably, and it is legitimate — but only if the finish reflectance is written into the specification and survives value engineering. Claiming the credit and then letting a dark membrane onto the roof is how a plant ends up 10% short on the hottest afternoon of the year. Default here is K = 1.00.

Course 1 · Module 1

Refrigeration cycle & psychrometric properties

Two instruments underpin everything that follows: the pressure–enthalpy diagram, which tells you what the machine is doing, and the psychrometric chart, which tells you what the air is doing. Learn to read both and the rest of the specialization is bookkeeping.

Vapour-compression cyclePsychrometry RefrigerantsComponents

The vapour-compression cycle

Four components, four processes, one closed loop. The refrigerant is not consumed — it is a courier, picking up heat where you don't want it and putting it down where you don't care. Everything you pay for is the cost of moving it uphill in pressure.

What each component actually constrains

Compressor
lift, capacity, part-load
Condenser

Expansion device

Evaporator

In Doha the condenser is the binding constraint almost every time. Ambient sets head pressure, head pressure sets compressor work, and no amount of evaporator selection recovers it.

Compressor types, and where each belongs

CentrifugalLarge plant, >300 TR. Best full-load efficiency; surge limits turndown.
Screw100–500 TR. Tolerates high lift — a real advantage in Gulf ambient.
ScrollSmall packaged, VRF. Good part-load in multiples.
ReciprocatingLegacy and small industrial. High lift, higher maintenance.
AbsorptionHeat-driven. Only where waste heat is genuinely free.
The review question that catches most chiller submittals

A vendor quotes COP or kW/TR at AHRI 550/590 standard conditions — 29.4 °C entering condenser water. Worked example D in Course 2 arrives at 40.6 °C entering condenser water for a Doha tower on a design day. That is an 11 K gap, and chiller power rises steeply with condenser temperature. If the submittal does not carry a performance point at your condenser water temperature, the efficiency number in it is not the efficiency you will buy.

Reading the psychrometric chart

The chart is a two-property map. Fix any two independent properties and every other one is determined — that is the whole idea. Below is the real chart with this guide's Doha state points plotted on it, so the abstractions have somewhere to land.

The number that decides your coil

Per litre per second of ventilation air, relative to the room: Case 1 removes 11.5 g/h of moisture (a latent credit of 8.0 W per L/s), while Case 2 adds 58.3 g/h (a latent load of 40.5 W per L/s). Same building, same city, same code-required ventilation rate — and the latent duty swings by a factor of five and changes sign. Any load calculation that runs only the 45 °C condition has not seen the case that sizes the coil.

Module 1 — practice questionsNot started
1

On a pressure–enthalpy diagram, the expansion process is drawn as a vertical line. Why?

Throttling is an isenthalpic process — no shaft work, negligible heat transfer, so h₄ = h₃. Entropy increases (it is irreversible), which is why the expansion device is the one loss you design in deliberately.
2

A chiller submittal states 0.58 kW/TR with no qualifying condition. Which entering condenser water temperature does AHRI 550/590 use for nominal rating?

29.4 °C (85 °F). Worked example D lands a Doha tower at 40.6 °C entering condenser water on a design day — an 11 K gap. Chiller power climbs steeply with condensing temperature, so a quoted kW/TR with no stated condition tells you nothing about what you are buying.
3

Doha Case 1 outdoor air holds 6.395 g/kg; the room is at 9.299 g/kg. What is the latent effect of ventilation air at this condition?

The outdoor air is drier than the room, so it dehumidifies the space — roughly 8.0 W of latent credit per L/s. This is why Case 1 produces a grand sensible heat factor of 1.00 and a dry coil, and why it cannot be the case you select the coil on.
4

Which pair of properties can not be used together to fix a state on the psychrometric chart?

Dew point is a direct one-to-one function of humidity ratio at a fixed barometric pressure — they carry identical information, so together they fix nothing. You still need a second, independent property such as dry bulb.
5

The apparatus dew point (ADP) is best described as:

ADP is the effective saturated surface temperature — extend the coil process line until it strikes the saturation curve. It is not the chilled water temperature: the water runs colder than the ADP because heat must cross a tube wall and a film to get there.
6

A coil's bypass factor represents:

It is the un-contacted fraction. More rows and tighter fin spacing drive it down — a 6-row coil typically sits near 0.10, which is what worked example C converges on (0.128). Casing leakage is a separate defect, and one worth writing into your inspection checklist.
7

Why is direct evaporative cooling a poor proposal for a Doha project?

Evaporative cooling trades sensible heat for moisture, and the trade only works where the wet-bulb depression is large. At Doha's dehumidification design condition the air is already near 80% RH. Note the trap: at the Case 1 condition the depression is 23.5 K and it would work well — which is precisely why proposals get built on the wrong design day.
8

Which compressor type is most often preferred for 100–500 TR duty in Gulf ambient, on the grounds of high-lift tolerance?

Screw compressors handle high pressure ratios without the surge limit that constrains centrifugals — valuable when a Doha condenser pushes head pressure up on a design afternoon. Centrifugals still win on full-load efficiency in large plant; the choice is a trade, not a rule.
9

Compute the enthalpy of moist air at 24.0 °C dry bulb with a humidity ratio of 9.299 g/kg. Use h = 1.006t + W(2501 + 1.86t).

kJ/kg dry air
h = 1.006(24.0) + 0.009299(2501 + 1.86×24.0) = 24.144 + 23.669 = 47.81 kJ/kg (20.56 Btu/lb). Note that the latent term is nearly half the total at ordinary room conditions — moisture is not a rounding error.
10

Air at 45.0 °C has a vapour pressure of 1.03120 kPa. Saturation pressure at 45.0 °C is 9.59322 kPa. What is the relative humidity, in percent?

%
RH = p_w / p_ws = 1.03120 / 9.59322 = 10.75%. Relative humidity is a ratio of pressures, not of moisture masses — which is why it falls as air is heated even though no water leaves.

Course 1 · Module 2

Heat load calculation

The load is the deliverable everything downstream depends on. Get it wrong high and you buy plant that never modulates properly; get it wrong low and you own the problem for the life of the building. This module builds it component by component, twice — once at each Doha design condition.

CLTD / CLF / SCLASHRAE 62.1 Sensible vs latentRoom vs coil load

Three distinctions that must stay straight

External vs internal

External gains swing with sun and ambient and are what the CLTD tables address. Internal gains are largely constant while the building is occupied. Only external gains care what time it is.

Sensible vs latent

Sensible changes temperature, latent changes moisture. They are added separately and met by different coil behaviour. A coil can be perfectly sized on total duty and still fail to hold humidity.

Room vs coil load

Ventilation air is not a room load. In a mixed-air system it never enters the room unconditioned — it hits the coil. Put it in the room load and you will oversize airflow and undersize nothing useful.

The CLTD / CLF / SCL method

A solid wall does not pass heat the instant the sun strikes it. Mass absorbs, stores and re-releases the gain hours later, and the radiant fraction of any gain warms surfaces first and the air afterwards. The CLTD family handles both lags with pre-computed tables: an equivalent temperature difference for opaque surfaces, a solar cooling load for glass, and a cooling load factor for internal gains.

Opaque surfaces q = U · A · CLTD_corrected CLTD_corrected = (CLTD_table + LM) · K + (25.5 − t_indoor) + (t_outdoor,mean − 29.4) Glass — solar q = A · SC · SCL SC = SHGC / 0.87 Glass — conduction q = U · A · CLTD_corrected (no LM or K term) Lighting q = W_installed · F_ul · F_sa · CLF People q_s = N · SHG · CLF q_l = N · LHG (no CLF on latent) Infiltration q_s = 1.23 · V · Δt q_l = 3010 · V · ΔW U = thermal transmittance, W/m²·K A = area, m² LM = latitude/month correction, K K = surface colour factor (1.00 dark, 0.83 medium, 0.65 light) t_outdoor,mean = daily maximum − (daily range / 2), °C SC = shading coefficient SCL = solar cooling load, W/m² F_ul = use factor F_sa = special allowance (ballast/driver) CLF = cooling load factor SHG/LHG = sensible/latent gain per person, W V = airflow, L/s Δt in K, ΔW in kg/kg Method per ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals (1997), Ch. 28, Tables 30–34. The CLF rule that is most often broken

Cooling load factors below 1.0 credit you for heat still stored in the structure at the calculation hour. That credit is only real if the plant runs continuously. If the system shuts down at night, CLF must be taken as 1.0 — the stored energy has nowhere to go but into the morning pull-down. A Gulf office on a 06:00 start with CLF = 0.87 in the calculation is a building that will not hold setpoint until mid-morning in August.

Sanity check

31.8 m² per ton (342 ft²/TR) for a top-floor corner office with 40% glazing in Doha. Gulf offices with a QCS-compliant envelope generally land between 25 and 35 m²/TR, and worst-case corner zones sit at the tight end. This is inside the band and toward the efficient side — consistent with the low-E glazing (SHGC 0.28) and insulated roof (U = 0.30) specified.

If your own calculation returns 15 m²/TR for a similar space, you have almost certainly double-counted ventilation into the room load, used CLF = 1.0 on everything, or left the glazing at clear-single SHGC. If it returns 55 m²/TR, check whether the roof was omitted or the solar orientation swapped.

Why the two cases disagree, and which one wins

Case 1 produces the larger room sensible load — 8 225 W against 6 876 W — because the 45 °C ambient drives conduction hard. Case 2 produces the larger grand total — 10 621 W against 9 503 W — because outdoor air at 23.8 g/kg carries a latent load of 2 699 W where Case 1's dry air carried only 826 W total.

So the two cases size different things: Case 1 sizes the airflow, the ductwork and the fan. Case 2 sizes the coil and the chiller. Running only the 0.4% dry-bulb condition — the habit most load calculations fall into — undersizes the cooling coil by 11.8% and leaves the building unable to hold humidity on exactly the days occupants complain about it.

Why RTS replaced CLTD, and when to still use CLTD

ASHRAE withdrew the CLTD/CLF/SCL method from the Fundamentals handbook and replaced it with the Radiant Time Series. The reason is structural, not cosmetic.

The defensible position in a design review

Use CLTD as a hand check, not as the submitted basis. Its enduring value is that one engineer with a calculator can reproduce it and catch an order-of-magnitude error in a software output in twenty minutes. Its weakness is that its tables encode specific wall constructions and a specific base condition, and the correction terms stretch further the more your project departs from them — a Doha project already carries a +8.85 K correction before anything else. If a subcontractor submits CLTD as the primary basis for a large plant, that is a legitimate technical comment: ask for RTS or a validated software output, and keep your CLTD as the independent check.

Module 2 — practice questionsNot started
1

In a mixed-air system, where does the ventilation (outdoor air) load belong?

Outdoor air is mixed with return air upstream of the coil, so it is a coil load. Putting it in the room load inflates the supply airflow — the classic route to an oversized fan and an unnecessarily large duct riser.
2

Correct the roof CLTD for Case 1. Table value 35 K, LM = 0, K = 1.00, indoor 24.0 °C, mean outdoor 38.25 °C. Use CLTDcorr = (CLTD + LM)·K + (25.5 − ti) + (to,mean − 29.4).

K
(35 + 0)(1.00) + (25.5 − 24.0) + (38.25 − 29.4) = 35 + 1.5 + 8.85 = 45.35 K. The final term carries almost 9 K on its own — that is the distance between Doha and the base condition the tables were built for.
3

West glazing contributes 138 W/m² of solar load against south glazing's 47 W/m² in this July calculation. Why?

The west wall is in fact smaller (11.52 m² against 17.28 m²) and both use identical glazing. Solar geometry does it. In the Gulf, external shading and low-SHGC glass pay back on east and west and are close to wasted on south — the reverse of the temperate-climate intuition many textbooks teach.
4

When must cooling load factors (CLF) be taken as 1.0?

CLF < 1.0 credits heat still stored in the structure at the calculation hour. That credit only exists if the plant is there to remove it later. On a system that shuts down, the stored energy reappears as morning pull-down — take CLF = 1.0.
5

Solar gain through the west glazing: area 11.52 m², SHGC 0.28, SCL 430 W/m². Remember the CLTD method uses shading coefficient, where SC = SHGC / 0.87.

W
SC = 0.28 / 0.87 = 0.3218; q = 11.52 × 0.3218 × 430 = 1 594 W. Forgetting the 0.87 divisor and using SHGC directly understates the load by 13% — a common slip when mixing modern glazing data into a legacy method.
6

Which design condition sizes the cooling coil for this Doha zone, and by how much would the other one miss it?

Case 2 gives 10 621 W against Case 1's 9 503 W — an 11.8% margin, driven entirely by the outdoor-air latent load. Case 1 does have the higher room sensible load, and that is what sizes airflow. Different cases size different equipment; you need both.
7

Should a cooling load factor be applied to the latent gain from occupants?

Storage lag applies to the radiant portion of sensible gain warming surfaces. Moisture enters the air directly, so latent gain is instantaneous — no CLF.
8

ASHRAE 62.1 ventilation rate procedure: office space, 10 occupants, 96 m², Rp = 2.5 L/s·person, Ra = 0.3 L/s·m², Ez = 1.0. Find Voz.

L/s
Vbz = 2.5(10) + 0.3(96) = 25 + 28.8 = 53.8 L/s; Voz = Vbz/Ez = 53.8 L/s (114 CFM). Note the area term is larger than the people term here — which is exactly why demand control ventilation cannot turn the whole flow down (Course 4).
9

Why did ASHRAE withdraw CLTD/CLF/SCL in favour of the Radiant Time Series?

RTS is decidedly harder by hand — a 24-hour convolution per surface. That is the trade: transparency and applicability to real assemblies, in exchange for arithmetic. CLTD keeps its value as an independent hand check on software output.
10

A subcontractor's load calculation for a similar Doha office returns 15 m²/TR. What is the most likely cause?

15 m²/TR is roughly double the intensity this envelope should produce — an over-estimate, so look for loads counted twice or credits not taken. Omitting the roof would push the figure the other way, toward 55 m²/TR. Knowing which direction an error moves the answer is what makes the m²/TR check useful in review.

Psychrometry × heat load — selecting the coil

Module 2 produced watts. Watts do not buy equipment. This module converts the load into the four numbers a supplier actually needs: airflow, supply air condition, apparatus dew point and total coil duty — and shows why the two Doha design cases hand you two different answers.

RSHF · GSHF · ESHFApparatus dew point Bypass factorCoil selection

The three sensible heat factors

Three ratios, three different jobs. Confusing them is the most common error in this part of the syllabus, so anchor each one to the question it answers.

RSHF = RSH / (RSH + RLH) → sets the slope of the room process line GSHF = (RSH + OA_s) / GTH → describes the whole coil, mixed air to supply ESHF = (RSH + BF·OA_s) / (RSH + BF·OA_s + RLH + BF·OA_l) → the line whose extension gives ADP RSH, RLH = room sensible and latent heat, W OA_s, OA_l = outdoor-air sensible and latent, W GTH = grand total heat = RSH + RLH + OA_s + OA_l BF = coil bypass factor The ESHF line is drawn from the room state; where it strikes the saturation curve is the apparatus dew point. Classical Carrier construction, as taught in the heat-load syllabus. Standard

Worked example C — coil selection at the governing case

Carrying Example B's Case 2 loads forward: RSH 6 876 W, RLH 1 046 W, outdoor air 2 699 W, grand total 10 621 W. Supply air off the coil is taken at 13.0 °C.

Split the outdoor-air load into sensible and latent ṁ_oa = 53.8 L/s ÷ 0.8964 m³/kg = 0.06002 kg/s OA_s = 0.06002 × 1.0368 × (31.6 − 24.0) = 473 W   OA_l = 2 699 − 473 = 2 226 W
Sensible heat factors RSHF = 6 876 / 7 922 = 0.8679 GSHF = (6 876 + 473) / 10 621 = 0.6919 ESHF = (6 876 + 0.128×473) / (6 876 + 0.128×473 + 1 046 + 0.128×2 226) = 0.8390
Solve the supply state from the room energy balance ṁ_da·cp·(t_room − t_supply) = RSH and ṁ_da·(h_room − h_supply) = RSH + RLH → ṁ_da = 0.61120 kg/s, supply = 13.00 °C / 8.623 g/kg (92.5% RH, h = 34.85 kJ/kg)
Supply airflow V = 0.61120 × 0.8219 × 1000 = 502.3 L/s = 1 064 CFM (6.28 air changes per hour)
Mixed-air state — mass balance of 53.8 L/s outdoor with the balance return outdoor fraction 9.8% by mass, 10.7% by volume mixed air = 24.76 °C / 10.725 g/kg (55.0% RH, h = 52.23 kJ/kg)
Apparatus dew point and bypass factor from the process line extend mixed-air → supply to saturation: ADP = 11.27 °C BF = (13.00 − 11.27) / (24.76 − 11.27) = 0.128
Coil duty q = 0.61120 × (52.23 − 34.85) = 10.621 kW = 3.020 TR, coil SHR = 0.693

The classical construction, checked against the rigorous one

Drawing the ESHF = 0.8390 line from the room condition to saturation gives ADP = 11.47 °C. Solving the actual mass and energy balance and extending the real process line gives 11.27 °C. The classical graphical method is within 0.20 K here — comfortably good enough for selection, and a fair reason to trust the chart construction the course teaches. The rigorous route is used above only because it closes the energy balance exactly, which lets every number in this guide be checked against every other.

What a dry coil means for the specification

At Case 1 the coil sensible heat ratio is 0.998 — the outdoor air is dry enough to absorb the entire occupant latent gain on its own, and almost no condensate forms. That is comfortable, but it has three consequences worth writing down:

  • The apparatus dew point construction degenerates. With the process line nearly horizontal, ADP and bypass factor stop being meaningful selection quantities at this condition. Select on Case 2.
  • Humidity control is unavailable when you might want it. A dry coil cannot dehumidify. Any space needing tight humidity (archives, laboratories, some clean areas) needs a separate strategy, not a bigger coil.
  • Condensate drainage is still not optional. The plant runs wet for most of the year — the coil is only dry at this specific design extreme.
Selection verdict — carry these forward to Course 2

Fan, duct and VAV box: 601 L/s (1 274 CFM) from Case 1. Coil and chiller: 3.02 TR (10.62 kW) from Case 2, at ADP 11.27 °C and coil SHR 0.693. Specify both duties on the equipment schedule. A supplier given only the total tonnage will select a coil with a sensible heat ratio near 0.75–0.80 by default, and it will not hold the room at 50% RH on a September morning in Doha.

Module 3 — practice questionsNot started
1

Which sensible heat factor is used to locate the apparatus dew point?

ESHF. It accounts for the fraction of outdoor air that bypasses the coil surface and reaches the room unchanged, which is precisely the portion that behaves like a room load. Draw the ESHF line from the room state; where it meets saturation is the ADP.
2

Mixed air enters a coil at 24.76 °C, leaves at 13.00 °C, and the apparatus dew point is 11.27 °C. Compute the bypass factor.

dimensionless
BF = (t_leaving − ADP) / (t_entering − ADP) = (13.00 − 11.27) / (24.76 − 11.27) = 1.73 / 13.49 = 0.128. Typical of a 6-row coil. Fewer rows raise it; a 4-row coil near 0.20 would not reach this supply condition from this mixed-air state.
3

At Case 1 the coil sensible heat ratio is 0.998. What follows?

A dry coil. The 45 °C outdoor air is so dry it absorbs the occupant latent gain unaided. Drainage is still required — the plant runs wet for most of the year; the coil is only dry at this one design extreme.
4

Dry-air mass flow is 0.61120 kg/s and the supply air specific volume is 0.8219 m³/kg. What is the supply airflow in L/s?

L/s
V = 0.61120 × 0.8219 × 1000 = 502 L/s (1 064 CFM). Note that airflow must be converted through the supply specific volume, not the room's — at 13 °C the air is denser, and using the room value would overstate the volume by about 4%.
5

A supplier is given only "3.02 TR" for this coil. What is the most likely failure mode?

Total duty alone does not define a coil. A schedule must carry entering and leaving conditions (or duty plus SHR), airflow, and the chilled water temperatures. This is a standard finding when reviewing equipment schedules — and it is far cheaper to catch at schedule stage than at commissioning.
6

Why does this guide solve the supply state from a mass and energy balance rather than reading it off the ESHF construction?

Both work. ESHF gives ADP 11.47 °C against the rigorous 11.27 °C — a 0.20 K difference, immaterial for selection. The balance route was chosen so that coil duty and grand total heat reconcile exactly, which is what lets errors elsewhere in the guide be detected.
7

Between Case 1 and Case 2 the outdoor-air latent load moves from −440 W to +2 226 W. What must the air system be able to do?

Airflow falls from 601 to 502 L/s while coil duty rises from 2.70 to 3.02 TR. Increasing outdoor air at Case 2 would make it worse — outdoor air is the source of the moisture, which is exactly the reasoning behind demand control ventilation in Course 4.
8

Room sensible heat is 6 876 W and room latent heat is 1 046 W. Compute RSHF.

dimensionless
RSHF = 6 876 / (6 876 + 1 046) = 6 876 / 7 922 = 0.8679. Note this is materially higher than the coil SHR of 0.693 — the difference is the outdoor-air latent load, which the coil carries but the room never sees.
